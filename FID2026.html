Fall 2026 Faculty Institute Day

The Engaged Classroom: Connection, Challenge, and Care

Wednesday, August 19, 2026 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM Gwendolyn Brooks Library

Register to attend

Morning in the Atrium

4th Floor Atrium. Select a session to read more.

  • 8:30 to 9:00 AM Check-In and Breakfast Start your day with us 4th Floor Atrium check-in  |  Breakfast in the Sunroom
  • 9:00 to 9:35 AM Welcome and Opening Dr. Brandle Blakely, President Z. Scott, and Provost Dr. Sonja Feist-Price 4th Floor Atrium
  • This keynote addresses the central question of the day: what makes a classroom engaging, and what can faculty change without redesigning a course from scratch. Drawing on research on active learning and inclusive classroom spaces, we describe how this type of classroom supports student success. We will discuss approaches that faculty can adopt and how practices can be enhanced with Learning Assistants and the LA Model. The session sets up the strands that run through the rest of the day, including active learning, feedback that motivates, and academic challenge paired with structure.
  • New to this year's program: VitalSource will share practical options for making course books and learning resources accessible to every student from the first day of class, so that cost and access are never barriers to getting started. Faculty will see how to connect students to their required materials on day one and support equitable access across their courses.
  • This session examines how the Cougar Commitment supports students across the full lifecycle, from recruitment and onboarding through persistence, completion, and post-graduation success. Participants will look at how the Cougar Commitment aligns with key touchpoints in the student experience and the role faculty play in fostering belonging, engagement, academic success, and retention. It is designed to be interactive, helping faculty see where their work intersects with the broader student success ecosystem.
  • A guided table discussion during the second half of lunch. Rather than a presentation, faculty will talk through how AI is showing up in their own courses, what they have tried, and where they are stuck. Prompts will be provided at each table so groups can begin without a facilitator at every seat.

Closing

4th Floor Atrium

  • The closing session connects the theme back to course design. Participants will look at how course structure, pacing, and assignment design either invite engagement or work against it, and will identify one change to carry into the coming term. The session is intended to send faculty out with something concrete rather than a summary of the day.
