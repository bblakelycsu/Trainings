What to Expect

Before your exam starts, Integrity Advocate (IA) will ask you to turn on your webcam, show your ID, and scan your room. During the exam, IA monitors your session in the background. A real person reviews every session within 24 hours afterward.

The link below walks you through the entire process step by step so you know exactly what to expect.

Take a Practice Session

Try it before exam day

Use the official practice link to test your camera, microphone, and browser. This does not count as a real exam attempt and nothing is recorded. If something isn't working, you'll find out here instead of during your real exam.

Open Practice Session (opens in new tab)
https://www.integrityadvocateserver.com/Demo?level=6
  1. Free up your camera. Close any other applications using your camera (Zoom, Teams, FaceTime) and close other browser tabs.
  2. Check browser permissions. Click the Lock icon in the address bar, find Camera, and set it to "Allow" or click "Reset Permissions." Refresh the page.
  3. Clear your cache. Clear your browser cache or perform a hard refresh, then try again.
  4. Check device settings. Make sure your operating system has allowed the browser to access the camera in your privacy settings.
  5. Try a different device. Work-managed computers often have strict firewalls. If possible, switch to a personal computer.
3

Exam Day Checklist

  • Working webcam and microphone — Lab or desktop computers without a built-in camera will not work with IA.
  • Google Chrome browser — Highly recommended. Screen monitoring and full-screen enforcement work best in Chrome and Chromium-based browsers (such as Edge).
  • Your ID ready — CSU Student ID or a government-issued ID (state ID, driver's license, or passport).
  • Stable internet connection — At least 3 Mbps up/down. Wired connections are more reliable than Wi-Fi.
  • AI browser extensions disabled — ChatGPT, Copilot, and similar extensions must be disabled or removed. You will be blocked from starting until they are off.
  • Close other apps — Close Zoom, Teams, Discord, and any remote desktop tools before starting. These will block your exam.

Your ID

You will be asked to show your ID on camera before the exam begins.

Can I hide sensitive details on my ID?

Yes. After taking the photo of your ID, look for the Edit button. Click and drag over any area you want to block (like your ID number or address). Release and the selected area will be covered. You only need to show your name and face.

5

If You Get a Violation Email

If you receive an email about a potential violation, it means there may have been activity during your session that didn't follow the rules set for your exam.

  1. Read the email carefully to understand what was flagged.
  2. Contact your instructor directly if you have questions or believe there's been a mistake. Your instructor is the only one who can decide what happens next.

Note: IA only provides the proctoring service. Any decisions about violations come from your instructor or institution.
